  "tcc:abstract-en": >
    This work presents a generic semantic model for compiling academic
    documents authored in Markdown. The proposal separates content,
    documentary relationships, and presentation decisions, allowing standards
    and institutional profiles to be applied without changing the authoring
    source. The adopted method combines an immutable document tree, semantic
    passes, validation catalogs, and an intermediate publication
    representation. The evaluation uses a complete undergraduate thesis with
    external, internal, and post-textual elements, as well as citations,
    references, illustrations, tables, and code listings. The results show
    that cover, title page, catalog card, approval sheet, dedication,
    acknowledgements, epigraph, abstracts, lists, and table of contents can be
    generated by a profile independent from the semantic core. Section and
    element numbers also remain derived data, preserving reuse of the same
    tree in different publication channels. This approach reduces coupling,
    facilitates regression testing, and keeps files readable outside the
    application. It also creates clear boundaries for future institutional
    variations while maintaining predictable diagnostics and reproducible
    outputs. The conclusion is that the proposed architecture provides a
    sustainable foundation for a local, interoperable, and extensible academic
    compiler without sacrificing the traceability required during the
    production, review, and long-term preservation of undergraduate research.

Documentos acadêmicos exigem estrutura estável e apresentação verificável,
como discute @silva2024.

## Objetivos

O objetivo é publicar um trabalho completo sem acoplar a AST à norma.
